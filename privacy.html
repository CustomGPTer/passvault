Privacy policy

Last updated 26 November 2026. This policy covers the PassVault app for Android (package com.jonjackson.passvault) and this website. PassVault is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Everything you put into PassVault is kept on your phone, encrypted. It is never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • PassVault does not hold Android's internet permission. It cannot go online, so it cannot send anything anywhere.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics.
  • Copies leave the app only when you make them: an encrypted backup, a copied secret or an autofilled login.

What stays on your phone

PassVault keeps your vault in one encrypted file in the app's private storage, left out of Android's cloud backups and device transfers. The whole vault, including names, usernames, notes, folders, tags, history, trash and settings, is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a random 256-bit key. That key is sealed by your master password (through Argon2id), by your recovery key if you make one, and, if you switch them on, by keys held in Android's secure hardware for fingerprint or convenience unlocking. Your master password itself is never stored.

Backups, copying and autofill

  • Backups are encrypted with the vault's password and recovery key at the time. If you save one to a cloud folder, that service's terms apply to the copy, which stays encrypted.
  • Copied secrets are marked sensitive and cleared after the time you choose, where Android allows. Apps you paste into, and your keyboard, are outside PassVault.
  • Autofill sends the one login you choose to the app or website you are signing in to.
  • CSV files you import are read on the phone and never changed; delete them yourself afterwards.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. Uninstalling PassVault deletes the vault and its unlock keys. Backups you saved elsewhere are yours to delete.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.
